What does temperature actually control?
- Temperature controls the probability of the tokens that comes up in the LLM's output.
Higher temperature means the tokens that were less likely to be picked up might have
a higher probability now. And vice versa.

**One precision: temperature doesn't directly "control the probability of tokens in the output." It changes the probability distribution used for sampling the next token.

What is sampling?
- Sampling is the process of selecting the next token based on the probability 
distribution.


Why doesn't temperature = 0 mean "the answer will be correct"?
- The answer is based on the evidence provided. Temperature merely affects
the token that will come up in the output and not the correctness of the output.
Thus it can still be wrong.

Why did your 0.5 experiment potentially produce the exact same answer three times?
- The probability distribution across the tokens were relatively less distributed.
Hence, everytime, the LLM ended up picking up the same tokens into the answer.


What changed at temperature 1.0 in your experiment?
- The probability distribution across the tokens became more uniform.
And the correctness of the answer remained the same but the answer itself
varied.

Does higher temperature necessarily mean lower accuracy? Why?
- Not really, because the correctness of the answer is dependent on the 
evidence provided to the LLM not on the temperature.

**Temperature can sometimes affect correctness indirectly because a different sampled sequence can lead to a different answer.

Based on today's experiment, would you use a high temperature for Sage? Why or why not?
- Yes, because the correctness will not be impacted but the answers will have a better variaion

** I would not choose high temperature for Sage based on this experiment.

Why?

Sage is an incident diagnosis tool.

We generally want:

Same evidence
     ↓
Stable diagnosis
     ↓
Predictable output

We don't particularly want:

Same incident
     ↓
Different evidence selection
     ↓
Different wording/conclusions

Variation is useful for some applications—creative writing, brainstorming, etc.

But for something like Sage, consistency is valuable.

Based on today's experiment, I'd tentatively choose something around:

temperature = 0.0

or another low temperature, not because 0.0 guarantees correctness, but because our goal is reliable, repeatable incident analysis.

And remember: this is a hypothesis based on a tiny experiment, not a universal rule.
